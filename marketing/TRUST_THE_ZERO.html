The first three seconds

Ten openings against the same footage. The constraint: the viewer must not know it's a product video until second seven.

"I gave an AI company one instruction."

Black, one cyan ring. Curiosity gap + implied autonomy. Strong, but "I gave an AI…" is now a saturated opening across the category.

"My AI company has made $0."

Black, one cyan ring, spoken flat and unbothered. Chosen. A founder volunteering failure in second one is a pattern interrupt nobody scrolls past — it reads as confession, not marketing, and the next four seconds prove it was neither.

Silent. Just the ring, breathing, for 1.5s.

No text at all until the dashboard detonates. Maximum novelty, highest risk — silence on a muted autoplay feed reads as a dead video.

"Every dashboard you've seen this week was lying."

Accusation hook, top-performing structure. Rejected: it attacks the viewer's own tools and starts an argument you then have to win instead of a story you get to tell.

"102 employees. None of them human."

Scale + uncanny. Strong, but it front-loads the claim your handoff doc says is the softest one you own.

"This number is the reason people trust it." — hard cut to $0

Contradiction hook. Very good. Held back as the A/B challenger.

"Watch what happens when I ask it for revenue."

Anticipation. Weaker: it promises a reveal and the reveal is a zero, so it reads as a letdown rather than a turn.

"It scored a website 100 out of 100. The website had no phone number."

Best pure-curiosity line here. Reserved as the hook for the 120-second developer film — needs 20 seconds to pay off.

"I built the only AI dashboard that's allowed to disappoint me."

Emotional + novel framing. Slightly too clever; the word "allowed" makes viewers re-read instead of watch.

"Nine months. 417 tests. $0."

Rhythm and specificity. Excellent for LinkedIn and X where the audience reads numbers; too dense for TikTok's first second.

Why the chosen hook works. Three mechanisms stack in under a second: it violates the category script (nobody opens with their own failure), it creates an unresolvable question (why is he saying this proudly?), and it makes a promise the next beat can actually keep. The viewer stays for the explanation, not the product.

Tooling — and why most of this shouldn't be generated

I compared the current field. As of 2026, Seedance 2.0 and Kling 3.0 lead the quality leaderboards, Veo 3.1 leads on motion physics and ships native audio, Runway retains the best control surface via motion brushes, and Higgsfield is best understood as an aggregator — it licenses Seedance, Kling, WAN, Veo and Sora behind one interface with a ~70-preset camera library and favourable credit maths for volume work.

The recommendation you didn't ask for. Do not generate the product shots. Titan is already a 3D-heavy, volumetric, dark-mode command centre. Any generative model asked to render "a futuristic AI dashboard" will produce something that looks more expensive and is not your product — which fails the one requirement you set, that the audience recognise it as Titan. Generation earns its place in exactly three shots the product cannot film. Everything else is the browser. That is also why you have a finished cut today instead of a prompt list.

Consistency bible

Colour — from tailwind.config.ts

  • #05070d void — the only background
  • #0a0e1a panel · #16203a edge
  • #22d3ee cyan — the only accent
  • #34d399 emerald · #fbbf24 amber · #fb7185 rose · #a78bfa violet — division identity only, never brand
  • Amber is reserved for honesty statements. It never decorates.

Type

  • Mono for everything on screen. Titan has no second typeface and shouldn't get one for a film.
  • Labels: uppercase, 0.2em tracking, 10–11px equivalent.
  • Numbers: tabular, always. A number that shifts width as it updates looks fake.
  • No italics. No serif. No display face.

Camera

  • Pushes only. No orbits around UI, no handheld, no whip pans.
  • Constant velocity or a single ease-out. Never ease-in-out — it reads as a template.
  • Max push 1.3×. Beyond that the UI crops and stops being recognisable.
  • Cuts land on the beat. Dissolves only into and out of black.

Materials & light

  • Everything is emissive on black. No metal, no glass, no reflections — Titan is a HUD, not a spaceship.
  • Bloom stays subtle; the real app uses a restrained bloom pass and the film matches it.
  • Particles move slowly and sparsely. Swirling particle storms are the house style of every generic AI ad.
  • Grid: 44px, rgba(34,211,238,.04). Present in every frame that isn't pure black.

Voice

  • Male, mid-30s–40s, unhurried, close-mic'd, slightly dry. No trailer gravel, no upsell lift.
  • Falling intonation at line ends. He is stating, not selling.
  • Pauses are written into the script and must survive the edit.

Banned

  • Glowing blue brains, humanoid robots, circuit boards, code rain, neon cities
  • Any number not produced by the running product
  • The words revolutionary, game-changing, world's most advanced
  • The CONNECT rail, and the demo-mode figures

Sound architecture

72 BPM throughout — slow enough that cuts on the beat feel deliberate rather than rhythmic, which is what separates a product film from a trailer. Key: D minor, so the sonic logo can resolve to the tonic.

Instrumentation. A sub sine at 40–55Hz carrying all the weight; a single detuned analogue pad; granular processed room tone from the product's own UI clicks; one felt-piano note reserved exclusively for the black at 00:52. No drums until Act 5, and then only a single percussive stab per cut — never a loop.

Energy curve. 0–4s: sub only, ~15%. 4–11s: bass enters on the boot lift, climbs to 55% as the UI resolves. 11–15s: thins deliberately to a single sustained note, ~30% — the drop before the drop. 15.0–16.2s: absolute silence. 16.2s: one impact at 100%, then bed at 45%. 22–34s: three stabs, each a semitone up. 34–44s: full arrangement, widest stereo, 80%. 44–52s: narrowing. 52s: everything cuts. 52.9s: one felt-piano note. Silence under the URL.

UI sound design. Sample the product's real chime(), blip() and tap() from lib/sound.ts rather than a library. Metric cards tick in sequence, quantised to 1/16. Each refusal gets a short dry click — the sound of a door not opening.

Sonic logo. Two notes, D → A, on the felt piano with a long tail, under the omega. Four seconds total. Reuse it unchanged on every asset forever; that's what makes it a logo.

Every metric card had a plausible number available. Traffic could have shown a session count from a script I never installed. Pipeline could have summed opportunities nobody agreed to. Revenue could have shown $0.00, which looks like an amount rather than an absence.

Instead: cost is null. Latency is null when nothing was timed. An unaudited site reads "not audited", not 0. The forecaster refuses to project on thin data. There's a test that fails if the API catalogue ever claims more integrations than it has — 1,675 catalogued, 5 wired.

It cost me the demo. Every screenshot I'd want to post is full of zeros.

But I sell compliance audits. If my own dashboard inflates a number, nothing else I hand a client is worth reading.

102 digital employees, 12 divisions, 9 jurisdictions, 417 passing tests, $0 revenue. All of those are measured.

Comment magnet

The question is genuine and you don't know the answer, which is the only reason it works:

Does a product showing $0 make you trust it more, or just look unsuccessful?

It splits an audience cleanly and neither side is stupid. Founders will argue that honest metrics are a moat and that every inflated dashboard trains buyers to discount all dashboards. Marketers will argue, correctly, that social proof converts and that nobody buys from a counter reading zero. Engineers will bring their own stories about metrics that were quietly fabricated. Everyone has been on both sides.

Second-order prompt, once the thread is moving: "What's a number in your own product that you know isn't really measured?" That one produces confessions, and confessions produce threads.

What not to do: no "comment X for the link", no polls, no bait. The moment this reads as an engagement tactic, the honesty positioning — the entire campaign — collapses.

Running this from your phone

Already done, nothing needed from you. The 15-second cut is rendered and committed at marketing/video/ in both 1080×1920 and 540×960, alongside the shot stills and the two capture scripts.

To re-cut after any product change — from a Claude Code session, no desktop: node marketing/director.mjs against a running local instance. The edit is code, so it reproduces exactly and always reflects the current product.

WebM, not MP4. The ffmpeg available here is compiled with VP8 only — no H.264 encoder. WebM uploads fine to YouTube, X and LinkedIn. Instagram and TikTok prefer MP4: convert with any browser-based converter (CloudConvert works from a phone), or run ffmpeg -i in.webm -c:v libx264 -crf 20 -pix_fmt yuv420p out.mp4 anywhere with a normal ffmpeg build. DESKTOP REQUIRED only if you want to grade or add music in an editor — CapCut mobile handles both, and is the path I'd take.

Audio. The cut is silent by design; captions carry it and most feed views are muted. Add the sound bed in CapCut on the phone using the architecture in section 09.

The one product fix worth making first: the Universe view's overlapping labels at 393px. It is your most-shown screen, it's your worst mobile frame, and every future piece of marketing runs into it.
